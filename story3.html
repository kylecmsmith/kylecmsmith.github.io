Sharvara and Shyama

India

In Hindu mythology, Sharvara and Shyama are the two four-eyed dogs of Yama, the god of death and ruler of the afterlife. Mentioned in the Rigveda, the pair serve as guardians of the path traveled by the souls of the dead, watching over the boundary between the human world and the realm beyond. Their names are often interpreted as meaning something like “Spotted” (Sharvara) and “Dark” or “Black” (Shyama), and they are described as vigilant, supernatural hounds who roam among humans while also guiding the deceased safely to Yama’s domain. Like many mythological dogs associated with death and liminality, they embody both fear and protection: they are guardians of cosmic order rather than purely malevolent creatures.
